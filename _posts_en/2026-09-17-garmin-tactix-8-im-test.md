## A tactical multisport smartwatch in the field

![Garmin tactix 8](https://www.geartester.de/uploads/url?path=%2Fgeartester%2Farticles%2F20260829__DLS9305_b0c681.jpg&width=1920)

## Introduction

In February 2025, Garmin brought the latest generation of its tactical multisport smartwatch, the tactix 8, to market. The line targets users with particular demands around ruggedness, navigation, and specialized extras like the Applied Ballistics calculator, which is exactly why I wanted to take a close look at it as a hunter. For four months, I wore the 51mm Solar variant with AB Quantum in the Ultralight license on my wrist. In this review, I'll introduce the watch, summarize the key specs, and walk through the features relevant to us as hunters, along with my own hands-on impressions from the field.

## Model lineup and variants

The tactix 8 comes in several variants that differ in case size, display technology, and Applied Ballistics license:

- tactix 8 AMOLED, 47mm: AMOLED touch display, more compact build, €1,399.99
- tactix 8 AMOLED, 51mm: same display tech in a larger case, €1,499.99
- tactix 8 Solar, 51mm with AB Ultralight: transflective MIP display with solar charging lens, €1,399.99
- tactix 8 Solar, 51mm with AB Elite: as above, with the expanded Applied Ballistics Elite solver, €1,549.99

## Design and build quality

The case is fiber-reinforced polymer with a titanium bezel and DLC coating; my Solar variant uses scratch-resistant Power Sapphire glass. A side sensor guard and waterproof, inductive buttons make the watch easy to operate even in rough conditions. MIL-STD-810 certification gives me confidence it can easily shrug off knocks, temperature swings, and water.

I find wearing comfort very good. The watch sits comfortably on the wrist even over long stretches, I even wore it continuously through sleep without it bothering me. Anyone with a very slim wrist should know, though, that the 51mm variant can feel noticeably bulky.

One small thing I noticed: the lug gaps have a bit of a gap with fairly sharp edges, which likes to snag the odd thread out of chunky wool sweaters in particular. Not dramatic, but worth mentioning.

## Controls: touch and buttons

The tactix 8 can be operated both via the touchscreen and entirely via the physical buttons. I particularly like that touch input can be switched on and off with a button shortcut, useful for avoiding accidental input with wet hands, gloves, or in dense brush. In the field, I used almost exclusively the buttons rather than touch, and that worked reliably throughout, gloves included.

## Display

On my Solar variant with the transflective MIP display, legibility stands out as one of the tactix 8's biggest strengths: in every lighting condition, whether blazing sun or dusk, I could read everything clearly, and the viewing angle impressed me consistently too. Color rendering is naturally less vivid than on the AMOLED model, but in exchange the MIP display wins on much lower power draw and solar charging capability. Given battery life, solar capability, and legibility in any light, I clearly prefer the MIP display over AMOLED. Also interesting for hunting use is the dedicated night vision mode, which switches between day and night views and stays legible even with night vision gear.

## Battery life

For me, battery life is a genuine standout feature of the tactix 8, for a full-fledged smartwatch with a touchscreen, voice calls, and the full feature set, it's hard to match. Sure, how long the battery actually lasts depends heavily on your own usage, but over my four-month test period, the battery never once ran completely dry on me. Occasional top-up charging proved practical: just five minutes on the charging cable adds around 5% battery, or roughly two extra days of runtime, which is usually enough to get me through further legs without needing to set the watch aside for a full charge for long. Where many classic smartwatches need an outlet after a day or two for me, the tactix 8 keeps going for weeks even under heavy use, and that makes the decisive difference for me in the field.

## Navigation and GPS

The tactix 8 uses multi-frequency GNSS reception combined with Garmin's SatIQ technology, which dynamically adapts the reception mode to the surroundings to balance accuracy and battery life. I was able to use the preloaded TopoActive maps for Europe from day one, with no need to buy extra maps; further map packages can be downloaded over WiFi if needed. Features like waypoint projection, dual position GPS format, and Around Me navigation are especially useful to me when I'm off marked trails. In practice, the GPS fix impressed me: I consistently got a signal in under a minute, and positioning was genuinely very accurate.

For me, navigation is the real game changer of the tactix 8. The decisive advantage over a smartphone: I don't even need to pull it out in the field. Especially in the dark, when I want to work with as little light as possible, the watch plays to its strengths, along with noticeably longer battery life than a phone and a more rugged build. For me, that makes navigation via the tactix 8 clearly superior to a smartphone for hunting use.

## Dive computer function

I also tested the tactix 8 as a dive computer. The watch is qualified as a dive computer down to 40 m; dive deeper than 45 m and the watch stops showing depth (until you're back shallower than 45 m), after which it enters a 24-hour deco lockout mode that can't be disabled. It reliably covered all the basic functions of a dive computer. Compared to dedicated Garmin dive computers, though, it lacks air integration, so it very likely won't fully replace a dedicated dive computer for serious divers.

## Tactical features: relevance for hunting

A central selling point for me as a hunter is the preloaded Applied Ballistics solver: based on wind speed, distance, and other parameters, it calculates a bullet's trajectory and helps me dial in my scope for longer distance shots. The solver is in regular use for me and has worked flawlessly every time so far. Through the AB Quantum app, I can manage rifle and ammunition profiles and use my smartphone as a secondary display.

Other tactically oriented features unique to the tactix 8:

- LED flashlight with white and green light (night-vision compatible) for orientation at dusk and while field dressing
- Night vision mode for the display
- Stealth mode: disables GPS storage, sharing, and wireless connectivity
- Kill switch to fully wipe user storage at the press of a button
- Rucking activity profile to track effort while carrying extra weight (e.g. a backpack or harvested game)

I found one criticism with the LED flashlight: the green LED is noticeably brighter for me than the dimmest setting of the white light. I would have liked either an additional, dimmer light level, or separate red and green LEDs, to dial in light intensity more finely.

Anyone considering upgrading from the preloaded Ultralight license to Elite (the upgrade costs around 250 US dollars online and can be done any time after purchasing the watch) should know what that gets you. The Ultralight license already covers the basics: muzzle velocity, temperature, humidity, air pressure, altitude, ballistic coefficient, wind direction, target incline, muzzle velocity calibration, zero range, and a range card. The Elite license adds significantly more for shooters at longer distances: a second wind speed input, spin drift correction, target/barrel height, aerodynamic jump correction, both proprietary and custom drag models (CDM/PDM), drop scale calibration, zero offset, a muzzle velocity temperature table, sight scale factoring, and a lead calculation for moving targets. Also practically important: Elite allows up to 30 saved and transferable profiles instead of just one, and the return-to-target function reaches 5,000 meters (30 seconds of flight time) instead of a maximum of 800 meters on Ultralight.

For classic stand and stalking hunts at typical shooting distances, the Ultralight license is entirely sufficient for me personally. Anyone who regularly shoots at long range or under changing conditions, though, will notice a real benefit from the Elite upgrade.

On price, the direct comparison is worth it for me: the 51mm Solar variant with AB Elite costs €1,549.99 from the factory versus €1,399.99 for the Ultralight version, a €150 premium. A later online upgrade from Ultralight to Elite, by contrast, runs around 250 US dollars. Anyone who knows from the start they'll want the Elite feature set comes out clearly ahead buying the higher-tier watch upfront rather than upgrading later.

## Health and fitness

The Gen 5 sensor package delivers round-the-clock data on heart rate, blood oxygen, and stress level. On top of that comes sleep analysis with a morning report, training readiness, an endurance score, and over 80 sport apps. For me, the fitness features aren't just a gimmick in everyday hunting life, they bring genuine value here too, for instance when I want to gauge my exertion on long stalks or in the mountains.

## Connectivity and smart features

I connect the tactix 8 to my smartphone via Bluetooth, so I see notifications right on the watch, and I can also make calls and receive smart notifications through it. I pay contactless with Garmin Pay, and Garmin Music stores playlists from popular streaming services (so far unfortunately "only" Spotify, Amazon Music, Deezer, and YouTube Music, Apple Music isn't supported). For emergencies out in the field, I use LiveTrack, and paired with an additional inReach device, I can also send an SOS distress signal over the Iridium satellite network with no cell coverage.

The tactix 8 doesn't have its own cellular internet connection, something the Fenix 8 Pro offers within the Garmin lineup. The trade-off: the Fenix 8 Pro only manages roughly half the tactix 8's battery life with that extra capability.

What genuinely won me over is the integration into the Garmin ecosystem. Via the QuickShare function, I exchange waypoints and data seamlessly between compatible Garmin devices, for example with the Xero L60i rangefinder, which also supplies Applied Ballistics data and links directly with the tactix 8. I cover how well that interplay actually works in practice in detail in my [review of the Garmin Xero L60i](/en/reviews/2026/07/03/garmin-xero-l60i-im-test/).

I'm less enthusiastic about the Garmin Connect app. It's extremely feature-rich functionally, but the interface feels heavily nested to me and looks pretty dated by now, especially compared to more modern fitness and outdoor apps. A visual and structural update feels overdue to me here. That said, this could of course simply be delivered later via an app update.

The watch's feature set is so vast that I'm still discovering new functions even after months, I doubt anyone has truly tried every single feature of the tactix 8.

## Value for money and market context

At €1,399.99 for the cheapest variant, the tactix 8 is clearly an expensive device for me, and it deliberately doesn't compete with classic smartwatches like the Apple Watch, it's too specialized and too high priced for that. For that price, though, you also get an enormous feature set and, above all, a watch that genuinely lasts, both in terms of battery life and build quality and ruggedness over time.

For comparison: the "civilian" Fenix 8 in 51mm with Solar costs €999.99, €400 less than the tactix 8 in a comparable configuration. Whether the tactical extras, like the Applied Ballistics solver, night vision mode, or stealth mode, are worth that premium to you is ultimately something everyone has to decide for themselves.

## Hands-on from a hunter's perspective

Over the entire test period, navigation was the function I used most often, whether sitting on stand, stalking, tracking a blood trail, or in the mountains. I also used the LED flashlight regularly, for instance at dusk and for orientation in the dark.

For controls, I reached for the physical buttons almost every time: in the field, gloves included, I mostly used the buttons rather than touch, and that worked reliably throughout. Especially in cold, wet conditions or with gloves on, button control was the most reliable way for me.

Overall, I had the tactix 8 on my wrist practically every day during the test period, whether for sport tracking, hunting, on the boat, diving, or doing fieldwork. That versatility across such different use cases, without the watch ever hitting its limits for me, shows just how well thought out the overall package of ruggedness, battery life, and feature set really is.

## Conclusion

Anyone just looking for a simple smartwatch will, in my view, probably find the tactix 8 simply too expensive and too specialized. But anyone looking for a Swiss Army knife on the wrist, ideally already owning other Garmin devices it can link up with seamlessly, and willing to pay the corresponding price, gets a very comprehensive, rugged, and well thought out overall package in the tactix 8, one that's proven itself equally in hunting, sport, and general outdoor life for me.
