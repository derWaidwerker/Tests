### Bergara: precision from the Basque Country

Named after the Spanish town of Vergara (Bergara in Basque), the Bergara brand today stands for precision, reliability, and modern rifle design. What many don't know: the company's roots lie in historic Basque gunmaking, a region of Spain known for centuries of fine gunsmithing tradition. There, in northern Spain, Bergara established itself as a brand name by combining technical innovation with hands-on craftsmanship. Originally specializing in high quality barrels, the company quickly grew into a serious manufacturer of complete hunting and precision rifles.

Bergara deliberately positions itself in a price segment that's attractive for ambitious hunters and precision shooters, without pushing into the premium territory of classic luxury firearms. The rifles are meant to stay affordable while still meeting precision and performance standards otherwise often reserved for far more expensive systems. The B-14 series in particular exemplifies that ambition: it offers a high quality combination of a precision barrel, a reliable action, and a modular build at a price-to-performance level that challenges plenty of competitors in its class. Nicely summed up by the company motto, "Our barrels make the difference."

Today, Bergara produces a wide range of hunting and sporting rifles, from the classic bolt-action through modern carbon variants to specialized precision systems. The brand has established itself in the international hunting and shooting community and is, for many, a trusted name when it comes to reliable, precise, yet still affordable rifles.

The Bergara B14² CIMA CF, the subject of this review, fits squarely into that picture. It combines the company's decades of expertise with a modern, carbon-based lightweight concept, an approach that reflects both Bergara's heritage and its ongoing evolution.

At the heart of the B14² CIMA CF's construction is the Bergara CURE carbon barrel, which achieves a significant weight reduction through a combination of a steel core and carbon fiber wrap, without compromising shooting performance. The barrel is paired with a very light RTM carbon Sporter stock weighing only around 510 grams, along with an integrated SMC carbon mini chassis that provides high torsional rigidity and gives the rifle stability despite its low weight. Together, these components bring the B14² CIMA CF to a total weight of 2.6 kilograms. That makes it one of the lightest models in its class, offering hunters a significant weight saving in the mountains, while stalking, or generally in demanding terrain.

I tested the rifle in .308 Win, paired with the Leader SDK suppressor and Leader MJG-SX ammunition. More on those later, first, the rifle itself.

### Build quality, bolt action, and handling

![Detail view](https://www.geartester.de/uploads/url?path=%2Fgeartester%2Farticles%2FIMG_7918_c3e71f.jpeg&width=1920)

The metal surfaces of the Bergara B14² CIMA CF are Cerakote coated, making it resistant to weather, corrosion, and hard use. The carbon grip stands out especially favorably: thanks to its textured pattern, it offers a very grippy hold and feels genuinely pleasant overall.

The bolt action is remarkably smooth. It doesn't quite reach the level of a Sauer, but it's very soft, cleanly guided, and further refined by the fluted bolt. Some criticize the bolt's 90 degree opening angle online, but personally I didn't find it a negative.

As long as you're not using extremely low profile mounts, that shouldn't pose a practical problem for the vast majority of users.

From the factory, the rifle has a 5/8-24 UNEF muzzle thread, including a muzzle brake in the box. Thanks to the carbon jacket, the outside of the barrel isn't prone to corrosion. The action is based on the Remington 700 standard, meaning many components, from triggers to the stock, are easily swappable.

![Muzzle thread](https://www.geartester.de/uploads/url?path=%2Fgeartester%2Farticles%2F20251222__DLS6718_56b06c.jpg&width=1920)
*Muzzle thread and the included omnidirectional muzzle brake*

### Precision, trigger, and magazine options

The B14² CIMA CF's barrel comes with a sub-MOA guarantee. The trigger adjusts between 2 and 4 pounds, roughly 900 grams to 1.8 kilograms. Anyone who wants to can easily swap the trigger for an alternative, thanks to the Remington 700 system, there's a huge selection available.

Magazines are AICS pattern; for PRC calibers, a hinged floorplate magazine is available as an alternative, or any model can be converted accordingly. The .308 Win version ships with a 3-round AICS magazine.

The dual sling stud mounting points at the front are a nice touch: a bipod can be mounted on one loop and a sling on the other, for example. Anyone needing more mounting options can alternatively fit a Picatinny rail.

The stock is 100 percent carbon, cleanly made, extremely light, and sits excellently in the hand. The combination of a fairly classic stock silhouette with a modern carbon look is of course a matter of taste, personally I like it a great deal. Three spacers are included, letting you adjust length of pull to fit.

### Carbon barrel, heat, and ammunition choice

![Barrel after several groups](https://www.geartester.de/uploads/url?path=%2Fgeartester%2Farticles%2F1763816572424_100_c656c8.png&width=1920)
*Even after several strings of shots, the outside of the barrel is only warm to the touch*

One thing worth keeping in mind: carbon has poor thermal conductivity. While steel conducts at around 53 W/(m·K), carbon fiber only manages around 17 W/(m·K). That means the inside of the barrel can get very hot while the outside remains comfortable to touch. On one hand that's pleasant, on the other, there's a risk the barrel overheats without you immediately noticing.

Reports circulate about other carbon barrel manufacturers where precision drops off after several fast strings of fire. I haven't been able to observe that behavior with the Bergara so far. Several consecutive five-shot groups, with brief cooling periods between, were no problem. For hunting use and the occasional trip to the range, that's entirely sufficient. Anyone expecting sustained rapid fire should look at a different rifle, though.

The safety is a two-position design. In the "off" position, the rifle can't be put on safe, and the bolt can be opened in either position. That takes some getting used to. For blood-trail handlers crawling through blackberry bushes on their stomach, this trait can matter.

![Open bolt](https://www.geartester.de/uploads/url?path=%2Fgeartester%2Farticles%2F20251222__DLS6703_6bba0c.jpg&width=1920)
*Bolt open with the safety engaged, be sure to factor in the 90° opening angle when choosing a mount*

Before zeroing, the barrel needs a thorough chemical cleaning, and mounting the Picatinny rail (or other rails) needs to be done absolutely correctly. Many complaints about oversized groups ultimately come down to these two points. Worth a look: [Bergara's accuracy testing requirements](https://www.bergara.online/us/accuracy-testing/).

The rifle is fairly ammunition sensitive and calls for some willingness to experiment. The 1:10 twist generally favors heavier bullets, though. For practice purposes, I can recommend the reasonably priced 180gr SP from Sellier & Bellot. It's also worth noting that the carbon construction makes the barrel run thicker (21.5mm diameter), for over-barrel suppressors, check beforehand whether there's enough clearance.

## Leader SDK suppressor

![Leader SDK suppressor](https://www.geartester.de/uploads/url?path=%2Fgeartester%2Farticles%2FIMG_7919_4a2695.jpeg&width=1920)

The Leader SDK suppressor is a high quality aluminum suppressor manufactured by Roedale for Leader Trading. Sound reduction sits at around 30 to 34 decibels, and first-round pop is very low. With the plastic pilot removed, the suppressor also fits over the Bergara's carbon barrel without issue.

![Suppressor without pilot](https://www.geartester.de/uploads/url?path=%2Fgeartester%2Farticles%2F20251222__DLS6712_15314c.jpg&width=1920)
*Without the pilot cap, the suppressor fits well over the thicker barrel*

Since the rifle's muzzle thread is 5/8-24 UNEF and the suppressor uses M18x1, an adapter is needed. Thanks to different adapters, though, the suppressor can flexibly be used on other rifles too.

For cleaning, the Leader SDK opens with a disassembly tool. At just 340 grams, it fits the character of the B14² CIMA CF perfectly: light, compact, effective.

There's also an interesting detail not explicitly mentioned on the website: a stainless steel mesh is already used inside the Leader SDK. That delivers extra sound suppression, since the hot powder gases get turbulated and slowed more effectively, noticeably reducing first-round pop. Less cold air volume inside the suppressor also means a more even pressure buildup, a practically relevant advantage, especially since the first shot is usually what matters most in a hunting situation. For a suppressor at this weight and price point, that's a technically clean feature that shows the materials weren't just cut to a budget, there was real thought put into it.

![Stainless steel mesh inside](https://www.geartester.de/uploads/url?path=%2Fgeartester%2Farticles%2F20251222__DLS6716_0b195b.jpg&width=1920)
*The stainless steel mesh is visible on the inside*

Maybe a slightly odd criticism, or more of a remark: the suppressor's markings are positioned so that (at least on mine) they end up on the left side, and upside down at that. That obviously changes nothing functionally, but it would satisfy my inner perfectionist quite a bit if the text were readable. For most people, the suppressor ends up hidden under a cover sleeve anyway.

## Leader MJG-SX: lead-free hunting ammunition

![Leader MJG-SX](https://www.geartester.de/uploads/url?path=%2Fgeartester%2Farticles%2F20251222__DLS6722_8e12bd.jpg&width=1920)
*The Leader MJG-SX consists of a bullet manufactured by SAX and an engraved PPU case*

The lead-free ammunition I used was the Leader MJG-SX, produced by SAX in Germany. The bullet weighs 120.4 grains and shows a very flat trajectory. With a maximum point-blank range of 191 meters, the load stays well suited for hunting at longer distances too, delivering an impressive 2,180 joules of energy even at 200 meters.

![Leader MJG-SX in detail](https://www.geartester.de/uploads/url?path=%2Fgeartester%2Farticles%2F20251222__DLS6725_e30547.jpg&width=1920)
*The Leader MJG-SX in detail*

The hollow-point brass bullet has a polymer tip. On impact, the front section fragments into four petals, forming roughly 2.4 grams of fragments, while the rear part of the bullet acts as a solid, reliably providing an exit wound, and reliable blood trail at the point of impact. The one drawback: this load is so far only available in this single bullet weight, more options would be welcome long term.

## Conclusion

The Bergara B14² CIMA CF is a consistently ultralight bolt-action rifle for demanding hunters, and it delivers on that promise impressively. At just 2.6 kilograms system weight, with high rigidity, a sub-MOA guarantee, and a very high quality carbon build, it offers exactly the advantage you need in the mountains or on long stalks. The smooth bolt action, excellent build quality, premium Cerakote coating, and Remington 700 compatibility add up to a package that convinces both technically and practically.

Naturally, there are traits worth knowing about: carbon dissipates heat poorly, the safety is a bit unusual, the barrel demands careful chemical cleaning before zeroing, and the rifle is comparatively sensitive to different ammunition. But anyone who finds the right load, respects cooling times, and mounts everything correctly gets a very precise, easy-handling rifle that is thoroughly hunting-ready in practice.

The Leader SDK suppressor is an ideal complement to the Bergara, an extremely light, effective suppressor that doesn't distort the CIMA CF's character. The Leader MJG-SX ammunition delivers a flat trajectory, reliable exit wounds, and strong remaining energy well beyond 150 to 200 meters, ideal for modern hunting distances.

For an MSRP of 1,785 euros, you get an extremely light, rugged, and highly precise rifle that has nothing to hide from significantly more expensive carbon systems. Anyone wanting to cut weight without losing precision, and who enjoys modern carbon technology, will find a nearly unbeatable overall package in the Bergara B14² CIMA CF.
